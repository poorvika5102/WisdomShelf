Made to Stick

Why Some Ideas Survive and Others Die | By Chip Heath and Dan Heath

Part I: The Enemy & The SUCCESs Framework
1. Overcoming the Curse of Knowledge
Core Principle The villain of memorable communication is the "Curse of Knowledge"—once we know something, we cannot imagine what it is like not to know it, causing us to communicate in abstract, jargon-heavy ways.
Technique / Practice The SUCCESs Model — systematically structuring ideas to be Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, and Story-driven to guarantee mental stickiness.
Actionable Rule Translate complex expert knowledge into sensory language that a non-expert can immediately visualize and retain.
Part II: Capture & Anchor Attention
2. Simple, Unexpected & Concrete Communication
Core Principle A sticky idea strips away fluff to find its core core message, violates human expectation to create curiosity, and grounds abstract concepts in tangible, human experiences.
Technique / Practice Curiosity Gaps & Schema Anchoring — opening a gap in knowledge before filling it with an idea, and anchoring new ideas onto existing mental concepts (e.g., "a movie like Jaws on a spaceship").
Actionable Rule Avoid high-level abstractions; frame every pitch using concrete sensory nouns, real-world examples, and surprising facts.
Part III: Inspire Belief & Drive Action
3. Credible, Emotional & Narrative Impact
Core Principle People believe ideas tested by real-life experience, care about ideas that appeal to identity and emotion rather than statistics, and act on ideas embedded in narratives that provide mental simulations for action.
Technique / Practice Human-Scale Statistics & Story Simulation — transforming vast metrics into relatable human terms and telling stories that act as mental flight simulators for listeners.
Actionable Rule To motivate action, replace dry data with personal stories that tap into self-identity and emotional resonance.

Master Implementation Checklist

  • Find the Core: Strip your message down to a single, essential idea before presenting.
  • Create Curiosity Gaps: Highlight what your audience doesn't know before revealing the answer.
  • Use Concrete Language: Ground messages in specific actions, sensory details, and tangible human cases.
  • Tell Actionable Stories: Use short stories that show people solving problems rather than delivering lectures.
