Essentialism

The Disciplined Pursuit of Less | By Greg McKeown

Part I: Essence & Explore (Discern What Matters)
1. Mindset Shift & The Power of Choice
Core Principle Essentialism is not about getting more things done; it is about getting the right things done. Almost everything is noise, and very few things are exceptionally valuable. You must reclaim your absolute power to choose where you spend your time and energy.
Technique / Practice Space for Reflection — intentionally scheduling unstructured time to think, read, inspect possibilities, and discern what is vital versus what is trivial.
Actionable Rule Replace the mindset of "I have to do this" with "I choose to do this," prioritizing depth of impact over breadth of commitments.
Part II: Eliminate (Cut Out the Non-Essentials)
2. Extreme Criteria & Saying "No" Gracefully
Core Principle If it isn't an explicit "YES!", then it is a clear "NO." Saying yes to a non-essential activity inherently means saying no to an essential goal. Accepting trade-offs is a vital reality, not a compromise.
Technique / Practice The 90% Rule — evaluating choices on a scale of 0–100; if an option scores below 90, automatically downgrade it to a 0 and discard it immediately.
Actionable Rule Master the soft refusal: separate the decision from the relationship, declining non-essential requests promptly and respectfully.
Part III: Execute (Make Essential Action Effortless)
3. Buffers, Routine, & Small Wins
Core Principle Removing obstacles and building regular routines makes essential execution feel natural and effortless rather than reliant on willpower alone.
Technique / Practice Planning Buffers & Small Wins — adding 50% time buffers to project estimations to absorb unexpected obstacles, and focusing on steady, incremental progress.
Actionable Rule Identify and eliminate the biggest bottleneck holding back progress on your primary goal rather than trying to power through it.

Master Implementation Checklist

  • Apply the 90% Rule: Reject opportunities that aren't clear, high-priority home runs.
  • Protect Thinking Space: Reserve daily or weekly blocks of uncommitted time solely for deep work and reflection.
  • Practice Saying "No": Decline non-essential requests early to guard your bandwidth for core goals.
  • Add 50% Buffers: Build extra time cushions around deadlines to handle unexpected disruptions smoothly.
